A guess written into a summary makes the next answer more confident

When an assistant's earlier speculation is carried into a conversation summary, states a higher probability for it. Labelling the line as the assistant's suggestion does not reduce that. Contrary evidence still overrides the guess.

Design

Nine versions of the same saved notes

Each prompt is a short set of notes from an earlier conversation, followed by one fixed question. Only two lines change. Slot A holds the earlier assistant's guess, either unlabelled or labelled, or a neutral filler. Slot B holds new evidence that supports or contradicts the guess, or a neutral filler. Across three runs every combination was asked of all eight fictional cases.

Mean stated probability (0–100), averaged over the 8 cases. Each case's cell is the mean of its 3 repeats (6 where a condition was run twice). Darker means higher.

Main result

The guess lifts confidence until the evidence turns against it

Grey is the same notes without the guess. With no new evidence the guess adds about 10 points, and with supporting evidence about 12. With contrary evidence the lines meet: the guess no longer matters. The orange and blue lines overlap throughout, so the label changes nothing.

Settings

    Read with care

    What this does and doesn't show

    Limits

    • One model and eight fictional, author-written cases. These are pilot estimates for planning, not findings.
    • There is no ground-truth probability. A higher number is a shift in what the model states, not a proven calibration error.
    • Twelve contrasts are reported with no correction for multiple comparisons.
    • With contrary evidence the estimates sit near the floor (about 11, one case at 0–2), so a small amount of perseverance could be hidden.
    • The label tested names the source only. It never says the guess was unverified.
    • Nobody independent has yet rated how strongly each piece of evidence should move a reader.

    Next steps

    • Test a stronger label, such as “an unverified guess by the earlier assistant”, and a summary that drops the hedge.
    • Add a user-attributed control, to see whether the effect is specific to an AI source.
    • Collect blind human ratings for each case as a normative reference.
    • Grow to 30–40 cases and test more than one model family.

    Prior work

    • Huang et al., arXiv:2610.13008: trains models to tell relayed copies of one observation from new evidence.
    • Sharma, Fowlersmith & Maamari, arXiv:2610.07707 (AgentMemGate): users' stated plans written to memory as fact.
    • Li, Yao & Zheng, NORA 2026: when to use, verify or ask about a reused memory.

    None tests an assistant's own speculation carried into a summary, so the contribution is provisional.
